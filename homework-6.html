Expected counts and the chi-square test

H₀: training category and phishing outcome are independent. H₁: the two variables are associated. We use a significance level of α = 0.05.

Under independence, the expected count in each cell is its row total multiplied by its column total, divided by the total number of observations.

Eᵢⱼ = row totalᵢ × column totalⱼ / n
χ² = Σᵢ Σⱼ (Oᵢⱼ − Eᵢⱼ)² / Eᵢⱼ
Degrees of freedom = (rows − 1)(columns − 1)

Independence in probability and statistics

Independence in probability

Two categorical random variables X and F are independent when their joint probabilities equal the products of their marginal probabilities for every pair of categories.

P(X = xᵢ, F = fⱼ) = P(X = xᵢ) P(F = fⱼ)

Equivalently, whenever P(X = xᵢ) is positive, knowing X does not change the probability distribution of F. Independence is symmetric: knowing F does not change the distribution of X either.

P(F = fⱼ | X = xᵢ) = P(F = fⱼ)

Independence in the empirical distribution

In a dataset, probabilities are replaced by observed relative frequencies. Exact empirical independence requires each joint relative frequency to equal the product of the corresponding marginal frequencies.

Oᵢⱼ / n = (row totalᵢ / n)(column totalⱼ / n)
Equivalently: Oᵢⱼ = Eᵢⱼ for every cell

Our dataset does not satisfy exact empirical independence. The overall click frequency is 50%, whereas the conditional click frequencies are 70%, 50% and 30% for the None, Basic and Advanced categories.

Independence as a statistical hypothesis

Even when variables are independent in a population, a random sample will usually contain differences between observed and expected counts. Exact equality is therefore not required in a sample for the independence model to remain plausible.

The chi-square test evaluates the size of these differences relative to what the independence model predicts. A small p-value provides evidence against the null hypothesis, provided the sampling assumptions and the chi-square approximation are appropriate.

Failure to reject the null hypothesis does not prove independence. A small sample, for example, may not provide enough information to detect an existing association.

Two different uses of the word independent

Independence between X and F is the hypothesis being tested. Independence between observations is a condition of the usual test. These are different ideas.

For example, recording many responses from the same person may produce dependent observations, even if each response occupies a separate row in the dataset. The contingency table alone cannot reveal or resolve this problem.

Association does not establish causality

An association means that the distribution of one variable differs across categories of the other variable. A causal claim is stronger: it states that changing one variable would change the other.

In a real observational dataset, people with advanced training might also have more professional experience, different job roles or greater previous awareness of phishing. These factors could help explain differences in clicking behaviour.

A chi-square test does not control for these alternative explanations and does not identify the direction of causation. A carefully designed randomized experiment could provide stronger evidence about the effect of training.

In this homework, the counts were chosen deliberately. The observed association illustrates the method and cannot establish any real-world causal effect.

Results and conclusions

  • The dataset contains 120 observations, classified into three training categories and two phishing outcomes.
  • Conditional click frequencies are 70%, 50% and 30%, compared with an overall frequency of 50%. Therefore, the empirical distribution is not independent.
  • Under independence, all six expected counts equal 20. Pearson's chi-square statistic is 12.8, with two degrees of freedom.
  • The asymptotic p-value is approximately 0.001662. Under appropriate sampling assumptions, this would lead to rejection of independence at the 5% significance level.
  • Because these data were deliberately constructed, the calculation is illustrative rather than an inference about a real population.
  • Statistical association alone does not demonstrate that training causes a change in phishing behaviour.

Statistical significance does not by itself measure the practical importance of an association. Interpretation should also consider the observed percentages, sample size, study design and application context.

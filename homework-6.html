Statistical Independence

Contingency tables, conditional frequencies and the chi-square test of independence.

The bivariate dataset

Each observation represents one person and one response to a phishing message. X is the person's security-training category. F records whether the person clicked the link.

This is a deliberately constructed synthetic dataset. It illustrates statistical calculations and does not provide evidence about real training programmes.

These are empirical frequencies from a constructed dataset. Differences between conditional frequencies describe an association in these data. They do not establish causality or, by themselves, justify conclusions about a real population.

Expected counts and the chi-square test

H₀: training category and phishing outcome are independent. H₁: the two variables are associated. We use a significance level of α = 0.05.

Under independence, the expected count in each cell is its row total multiplied by its column total, divided by the total number of observations.

Eᵢⱼ = row totalᵢ × column totalⱼ / n
χ² = Σᵢ Σⱼ (Oᵢⱼ − Eᵢⱼ)² / Eᵢⱼ
Degrees of freedom = (rows − 1)(columns − 1)

The p-value uses the asymptotic chi-square distribution. For this table, there are two degrees of freedom, so the upper-tail probability is exp(−χ²/2). This shortcut is specific to two degrees of freedom.

The dataset was deliberately constructed rather than randomly sampled. The test calculation is an educational demonstration, not evidence about a real population or the causal effect of security training.

Interpretation and limitations

  • Uniform sampling of the valid scalars gives uniform sampling of the non-identity points in the generated subgroup. It does not imply uniform coordinate frequencies.
  • Opposite points share the same x coordinate: (n − k)B = −kB. A point with y = 0 is its own opposite.
  • Coordinate bits need not be balanced. Their distribution depends on the curve, the subgroup and the chosen encoding.
  • Pearson correlation measures linear association only. A zero coefficient does not establish independence or security.
  • Hamming distances describe this finite experiment. They do not prove the strict avalanche criterion or establish that scalar multiplication behaves like a cryptographic hash.
  • The toy curve is easy to invert by enumeration. These results do not measure the security of secp256k1.

Online statistics and sampling distributions

Updating mean and variance with Welford's algorithm, then studying their behavior across repeated samples.

Part 1 · Welford's recurrence formulas

An online algorithm updates its statistics whenever a new observation arrives. Here, “online” means processing data sequentially; it does not refer to an internet connection.

Welford's algorithm maintains the observation count, the current mean and the sum of squared deviations from the current mean. Previous observations do not need to be stored by the algorithm.

SSₖ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ (xᵢ − x̄ₖ)²

SS is the sum of squared deviations from the mean, not the uncentered sum Σxᵢ².

1. Updating the mean

Let xₖ be the new observation and x̄ₖ₋₁ the mean of the previous k − 1 observations. For k ≥ 2, the updated mean is:

x̄ₖ = [(k − 1)x̄ₖ₋₁ + xₖ] / k
x̄ₖ = x̄ₖ₋₁ + (xₖ − x̄ₖ₋₁) / k

Define δₖ = xₖ − x̄ₖ₋₁, the difference between the new observation and the previous mean. Then:

x̄ₖ = x̄ₖ₋₁ + δₖ/k

The new observation moves the mean by a fraction of its distance from the old mean. As k increases, each new observation has a smaller weight.

2. Updating the sum of squared deviations

Separate the new observation from the previous ones:

SSₖ = Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ⁻¹ (xᵢ − x̄ₖ)² + (xₖ − x̄ₖ)²

Since x̄ₖ = x̄ₖ₋₁ + δₖ/k, every previous deviation changes by −δₖ/k. Expanding the squares gives:

SSₖ = SSₖ₋₁ − (2δₖ/k) Σᵢ₌₁ᵏ⁻¹ (xᵢ − x̄ₖ₋₁) + (k − 1)δₖ²/k² + (xₖ − x̄ₖ)²

The sum of deviations from the old mean is zero, so the cross term vanishes. Also, xₖ − x̄ₖ = (k − 1)δₖ/k. Therefore:

SSₖ = SSₖ₋₁ + (k − 1)δₖ²/k² + (k − 1)²δₖ²/k²
SSₖ = SSₖ₋₁ + [(k − 1)/k]δₖ²
SSₖ = SSₖ₋₁ + δₖ(xₖ − x̄ₖ)

The first difference uses the old mean. The second difference uses the updated mean.

3. From SS to variance

The descriptive variance divides SS by k. The corrected sample variance divides SS by k − 1. Under independent, identically distributed sampling, the corrected version is an unbiased estimator of the population variance.

vₖ = SSₖ/k, for k ≥ 1
sₖ² = SSₖ/(k − 1), for k ≥ 2

Substituting the recurrence for SS gives the corresponding variance recurrences:

vₖ = [(k − 1)/k]vₖ₋₁ + δₖ(xₖ − x̄ₖ)/k, for k ≥ 2

sₖ² = [(k − 2)/(k − 1)]sₖ₋₁² + δₖ(xₖ − x̄ₖ)/(k − 1), for k ≥ 3

Initially, the first observation gives x̄₁ = x₁ and SS₁ = 0. The corrected sample variance is undefined with only one observation. For two observations, s₂² = SS₂.

4. The three-line update

Start with mean = 0 and SS = 0. For each observation x, let k be the number of observations including the new one. The core update is: 

const delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / k;
SS += delta * (x - mean);

After the update, the corrected sample variance is SS/(k − 1), provided k ≥ 2. The initial value mean = 0 is a computational convention: an empty sample has no statistical mean.

The algorithm requires constant memory for its running state. It also avoids the potentially unstable subtraction of large, nearly equal quantities in Σxᵢ² − kx̄ₖ².
